Core Concepts

Understanding Phishing

Phishing is a deceptive attempt to obtain sensitive information or persuade someone to take a harmful action.

How phishing works

  1. 1
    A deceptive message arrives

    It may look like it comes from a bank, employer, delivery company, or known contact.

  2. 2
    A trigger creates pressure

    The attacker uses urgency, fear, curiosity, authority, or reward to rush the target.

  3. 3
    The user is directed toward danger

    A link, attachment, request, or phone number may begin an attempt to steal data or money.

  4. 4
    Credentials or access are compromised

    Consequences can include identity theft, account takeover, fraud, or malware infection.

Possible consequences

›

Identity theft
Personal details are used without permission.

›

Financial loss
Fraudulent payments or unauthorized purchases occur.

›

Account takeover
Attackers gain access to email, social media, or work accounts.

›

Malware infection
A malicious file or link installs harmful software.

Types of phishing

E

Email phishing

Broad email campaigns sent to many people using a familiar brand or urgent request.

S

Spear phishing

Highly targeted messages designed around a specific person or organization.

SMS

Smishing

Fraudulent SMS or messaging-app texts that use links or phone numbers.

V

Vishing

Voice calls that impersonate support teams, banks, officials, or employers.

W

Whaling

Attacks aimed at executives or individuals with high-value authority or access.

C

Clone phishing

A legitimate message is copied and altered to contain a harmful link or attachment.

Red Flag Detection

Identifying Phishing Attacks

Pause and inspect the details. One or more warning signs should be enough to verify independently.

From: accounts@secure-bank.exampleToday, 09:12
BANKSECURE

Immediate action required: verify your account

Your account will be suspended in 30 minutes. Confirm your identity using the secure link below.

Verify My Account

Fictional training example — do not enter credentials.

Warning signs highlighted

  • !

    Suspicious sender
    The domain may resemble the real company but is not its official domain.

  • !

    Unrealistic deadline
    A 30-minute deadline creates fear and pressure.

  • !

    Unexpected request
    It asks you to verify personal or financial information.

  • !

    Generic greeting
    Legitimate organizations often personalize important notices.

  • !

    Urgency to click
    The message discourages careful verification.

Suspicious sender addresses

Check the full address, domain spelling, reply-to address, and whether it matches the organization's official domain.

Fake login pages

Look for slight misspellings, poor design, missing HTTPS, and unexpected prompts for a password or OTP.

Fraudulent URLs

Read the domain from right to left. Watch for extra words, hyphens, numbers, or look-alike characters.

Attachments and links

Be cautious with unfamiliar file types, unexpected invoices, ZIP files, shortened links, and document macros.

Human Psychology

Social Engineering Techniques

Attackers often manipulate human behavior instead of relying on a technical flaw.

01

Urgency and fear

“Act now or lose access” encourages rushed decisions without verification.

02

Impersonation

A caller or message pretends to be a bank, manager, relative, or trusted service.

03

Baiting

The attacker promises free software, exclusive content, prizes, or important files.

04

Pretexting

A believable invented scenario gives the attacker a reason to request information.

05

Authority manipulation

The message claims to come from a senior leader or government authority.

06

Reward-based scams

Unexpected refunds, gifts, money, or job opportunities tempt people to comply quickly.

04

Defense Practices

Practical Security Tips

Build a repeatable verification habit. Small precautions prevent many common attacks.

✓

Verify the sender

Use a known phone number or separate communication channel. Do not reply to a suspicious message.

✓

Inspect website URLs

Type trusted addresses manually or use a saved bookmark. Check the actual domain and HTTPS status.

✓

Protect OTPs and passwords

Never share codes or passwords, even if the caller claims to be technical support or a bank.

✓

Enable MFA

Use an authenticator app or security key. Avoid approving unexpected sign-in prompts.

✓

Keep files and links untrusted

Do not open unexpected attachments or links. Confirm with the sender through another method.

✓

Use strong, unique passwords

Use a reputable password manager and change passwords promptly after suspected compromise.

Real-World Scenarios

Case Studies

Study fictional examples inspired by common attack patterns.

CASE 01

Fake Bank KYC Update

Attack method: An email claims your account is blocked and directs you to a fake verification page.

Warning signs: Threatening deadline, unofficial sender domain, and a link requesting OTP and password.

Consequences: Account takeover and unauthorized financial transactions.

Prevention: Open the bank's official app or type its known website address yourself.

CASE 02

Fake Delivery Notice

Attack method: A text says a package cannot be delivered and asks for a redelivery fee.

Warning signs: Unexpected message, shortened link, small fee, and urgent action request.

Consequences: Card data theft or malware from a malicious checkout page or file.

Prevention: Track parcels only through the carrier's official app or website.

CASE 03

Fake Social Login Page

Attack method: A post offers a fake prize and sends users to a cloned social login screen.

Warning signs: Prize offer, unfamiliar domain, poor branding, and password field in an unsolicited page.

Consequences: Stolen login credentials and misuse of the victim's account.

Prevention: Visit the platform directly and never enter credentials through an offered link.

CASE 04

Fake Internship Offer

Attack method: A message claims you were selected for an internship and asks for identity details or a fee.

Warning signs: Job offer without an interview, free email domain, payment request, or attachment.

Consequences: Identity theft, financial loss, or data collected for further scams.

Prevention: Verify the recruiter and role through the organization's official careers page.
